# Problem Specification

## Observations

### O1: Mixed findings on attention in autism
- **Statement**: Autistic individuals frequently describe environments as noisy and hard to filter, yet experimental work sometimes reports typical or even superior perceptual performance.
- **Evidence**: Introduction, manuscript §1 (refs 1–8); debate framed as bottom-up capture vs. post-selective/decision differences.
- **Implication**: The locus of any attentional difference (capture vs. disengagement/decision) is unresolved.

### O2: Intact spatial-prior acquisition but heightened sensitivity to surprise
- **Statement**: Allenmark et al. (2021) found ASD and TD acquire probability-cueing spatial priors equally, but ASD show a post-selective "coincidence effect" ~5× larger than TD when a target appears at a previously rare-distractor location.
- **Evidence**: Manuscript §1 (ref 28).
- **Implication**: Localises the ASD difference to post-selective processing rather than initial capture, when priors are *learned from statistics*.

### O3: AIPU — typical priors, faster belief updating
- **Statement**: Shi et al. (2025) proposed the atypical iterative prior updating (AIPU) account: autistic individuals integrate priors and likelihood typically for immediate estimates but update priors more dynamically trial-by-trial, weighting new sensory input more heavily during *belief revision*.
- **Evidence**: Manuscript §1 (ref 29); convergent HGF/phasic-volatility (ref 30) and temporal-processing (ref 31) evidence.
- **Implication**: Predicts attentional differences in the *temporal unfolding* of selection, not in prior formation.

### O4: Distractor interference is suppressible with valid expectations in TD
- **Statement**: In additional-singleton paradigms a colour singleton slows responses ("distractor interference") unless observers can form expectations to proactively suppress it; explicit valid spatial cues reduce oculomotor capture in TD.
- **Evidence**: Manuscript §1 (refs 20–23, 35).
- **Implication**: A 100% valid spatial cue should shield TD; whether it shields ASD is the test.

## Gaps

### G1: Does the where/what dissociation persist under *explicit* perfect cueing?
- **Statement**: Prior dissociations relied on priors *learned* from statistical regularities; it is unknown whether they persist when spatial priors are supplied explicitly, trial-by-trial, via a 100%-valid cue.
- **Caused by**: O2, O4.
- **Existing attempts**: Probability-cueing designs (Allenmark et al. 2021) — learned, not explicit, priors.
- **Why they fail**: They cannot separate "failure to learn the spatial prior" from "atypical use after selection," because the prior must first be acquired.

### G2: Is the difference in iterative updating rather than prior formation?
- **Statement**: If the core difference is iterative updating, it should appear in the temporal dynamics of attention (search depth, target re-checking), not only in manual RT.
- **Caused by**: O3.
- **Existing attempts**: RT-only distractor studies.
- **Why they fail**: RT alone cannot dissociate initial capture from prolonged post-selective sampling.

## Key Insight
- **Insight**: Pair a **100% valid spatial cue** (removes spatial uncertainty → isolates prior *use*) with a **session-wise distractor-prevalence manipulation** (varies how *expected/surprising* the distractor is → frequency prior), and track eye movements to time-resolve capture vs. disengagement. If ASD reflects impaired spatial-prior use, ASD should show more *initial* capture; if it reflects updating-stage differences, capture should be equal but post-selective processing (more fixations, more target refixations) should be prolonged — and persist even under high prevalence.
- **Derived from**: O2, O3, O4.
- **Enables**: A stage-specific test that distinguishes AIPU (updating-driven, prevalence-insensitive) from strictly phasic precision-regulation (surprise-driven, prevalence-graded).

## Assumptions
- A1: A diamond-among-shapes additional-singleton task validly elicits distractor interference and oculomotor capture.
- A2: 1.5° ROIs around stimulus locations adequately classify fixations as target/distractor/other.
- A3: The 100% valid line cue is processed as a usable spatial prior despite instructions to ignore it.
- A4: ICD-10 confirmed diagnosis (F84.x) plus RADS-R screening yields valid ASD/TD group separation.
- A5: Counterbalancing session order controls for prevalence-condition order/practice effects.
