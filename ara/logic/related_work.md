# Related Work (Typed Dependency Graph)

Citation numbers refer to the manuscript reference list (`docs/manuscript-r3.md` §5). Full `RW`
blocks are given for works with a specific technical/theoretical delta; the broader citation
footprint is summarised below.

## RW01: Shi et al., 2025 — Atypical Iterative Prior Updating (AIPU)
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.bpsgos.2025.100468 (ref 29)
- **Type**: imports / extends
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Provides the core theoretical account being tested — typical prior formation but heavier sensory weighting during *belief updating*; this study extends it from perception (central-tendency task) to attentional control.
  - Why: To test whether updating-stage atypicality manifests in disengagement from salient distractors.
- **Claims affected**: C01, C04, C05, C06 (interpretation)
- **Adopted elements**: The updating-vs-integration distinction; the prediction of prevalence-insensitive prolonged engagement.

## RW02: Allenmark et al., 2021 — Priors in autism: where to look vs. what is there
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s10803-020-04828-2 (ref 28)
- **Type**: extends / baseline
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Showed intact probability-cueing (learned spatial prior) but a ~5× larger post-selective coincidence effect in ASD. This study replaces the *learned* prior with an *explicit* 100%-valid cue and adds time-resolved eye movements.
  - Why: To test whether the where/what dissociation persists with explicit, trial-by-trial priors.
- **Claims affected**: C03, C07
- **Adopted elements**: The pre- vs post-selective framing; effect size d = 0.33 used for power analysis.

## RW03: Theeuwes, 1992 — Additional-singleton paradigm
- **DOI**: 10.3758/BF03211656 (ref 20)
- **Type**: imports
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Supplies the base paradigm (colour singleton slows shape-target search).
  - Why: Operationalises distractor interference.
- **Claims affected**: C01
- **Adopted elements**: Task structure.

## RW04: van Zoest et al., 2021 — Strategic distractor suppression via cues
- **DOI**: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0553-21.2021 (ref 35)
- **Type**: baseline
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Demonstrated that explicit spatial cues reduce oculomotor capture in neurotypical observers.
  - Why: Grounds the prediction that TD should be shielded by the valid cue.
- **Claims affected**: C02
- **Adopted elements**: Cueing-based proactive suppression rationale.

## RW05: Lawson et al., 2014 / Van de Cruys et al., 2014 — Precision-regulation accounts
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fnhum.2014.00302 (ref 24); 10.1037/a0037665 (ref 26)
- **Type**: refutes / bounds
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Provide the competing precision-regulation framework; the present data (interference even under high/expected prevalence) fit phasic, surprise-driven variants less well than AIPU.
  - Why: To adjudicate AIPU vs. strictly surprise-graded precision-regulation.
- **Claims affected**: C05, C07 (interpretation)
- **Adopted elements**: Precision-weighting vocabulary; phasic vs tonic distinction.

## RW06: Pellicano & Burr, 2012 — "Weak priors" / hypo-priors
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.tics.2012.08.009 (ref 27)
- **Type**: refutes
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: The weak-priors view predicts more initial capture in ASD; the present comparable first-fixation capture argues against it at the level of spatial orienting.
  - Why: Directly contrasted with the data.
- **Claims affected**: C03
- **Adopted elements**: none (contrasted).

## RW07: Plank et al., 2025 — HGF / phasic volatility in emotion learning
- **DOI**: 10.31234/osf.io/zrhyw_v1 (ref 30)
- **Type**: imports (convergent evidence)
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Trend toward elevated phasic (not tonic) volatility in ASD — convergent with AIPU's selective-updating claim.
- **Claims affected**: interpretation (Discussion)
- **Adopted elements**: tonic/phasic dissociation as convergent support.

## RW08: Lambrechts et al., 2018 — Temporal processing in ASD
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.nicl.2017.09.023 (ref 31)
- **Type**: imports (convergent evidence)
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: Diminished neural specificity for temporal features despite intact behaviour — bottom-up dominance, convergent with AIPU.
- **Claims affected**: interpretation (Discussion)
- **Adopted elements**: cross-domain convergence argument.

## RW09: Pirrone et al., 2017 / Ratcliff & McKoon, 2008 — Drift-diffusion modelling
- **DOI**: 10.1037/neu0000320 (ref 51); 10.1162/neco.2008.12-06-420 (ref 52)
- **Type**: extends (future direction)
- **Delta**:
  - What changed: DDM/accumulator framing — Pirrone found wider boundary separation with intact drift in ASD; the manuscript proposes mapping AIPU onto larger effective evidence thresholds.
- **Claims affected**: interpretation (Discussion, future work)
- **Adopted elements**: boundary-separation interpretation of prolonged search.
