# Concepts

## Additional-singleton search task
- **Notation**: —
- **Definition**: A visual search paradigm (Theeuwes, 1992) in which observers search for a shape-defined target (here a diamond among non-diamond shapes) while an irrelevant colour singleton may appear; the singleton reliably slows target responses.
- **Boundary conditions**: Here, six items at equidistant locations 6° from centre; target contains a horizontal/vertical line, distractors oblique lines; target/singleton colours alternate cyan/orange.
- **Related concepts**: Distractor interference, Singleton presence, Oculomotor capture

## Distractor interference
- **Notation**: RT_present − RT_absent (also computed on number of fixations)
- **Definition**: The cognitive cost imposed by a salient singleton distractor, quantified per participant and condition as the difference between singleton-present and singleton-absent trials.
- **Boundary conditions**: Computed on correct, non-outlier trials; in this study expressed both as an RT difference (ms) and as a difference in number of fixations.
- **Related concepts**: Additional-singleton search task, Singleton presence

## Singleton presence (Singleton)
- **Notation**: absent / present (within-subject factor)
- **Definition**: Whether a uniquely coloured singleton distractor appeared on a trial.
- **Boundary conditions**: When absent, all items share one colour; the cue then points to a random location.
- **Related concepts**: Distractor interference, Distractor prevalence

## Distractor prevalence (frequency prior)
- **Notation**: low (50%) / high (90%) — between-session, within-subject factor
- **Definition**: The session-wise base rate of singleton-distractor presence; operationalises a frequency/volatility prior governing how *expected* a distractor is.
- **Boundary conditions**: Two counterbalanced sessions of 200 trials each (10 blocks × 20 trials).
- **Related concepts**: Singleton presence, Spatial prior, AIPU account

## 100% valid spatial cue (spatial prior)
- **Notation**: cue_validity = 1.0
- **Definition**: A white line cue presented before search that points with perfect validity to the location where the singleton distractor will appear (when present), supplying an explicit trial-by-trial spatial prior ("where not to look").
- **Boundary conditions**: When the singleton is absent the cue points to a random location; participants are instructed to ignore the cue.
- **Related concepts**: Distractor prevalence, Pre-selective processing

## Oculomotor capture / first-fixation capture
- **Notation**: proportion of first saccades landing on a location
- **Definition**: The tendency of the first post-central saccade to land on the salient singleton distractor; an index of involuntary, stimulus-driven early selection.
- **Boundary conditions**: First fixation = the fixation immediately following central fixation; classified via 1.5° ROIs.
- **Related concepts**: Pre-selective processing, Additional-singleton search task

## Pre- vs. post-selective processing
- **Notation**: —
- **Definition**: Pre-selective processing concerns where attention is initially deployed (capture, first fixation); post-selective processing concerns what happens after a salient item is selected (disengagement, re-checking, decision commitment).
- **Boundary conditions**: Operationalised here as first fixation (pre-selective) vs. number of fixations / target refixations (post-selective).
- **Related concepts**: Oculomotor capture, Disengagement

## Atypical iterative prior updating (AIPU) account
- **Notation**: —
- **Definition**: Account (Shi et al., 2025) holding that autistic individuals form priors and integrate prior+likelihood typically for immediate estimates, but update priors more dynamically trial-by-trial, weighting new sensory evidence more heavily during *belief revision*.
- **Boundary conditions**: The atypical weighting is specified at the updating stage, not the perceptual-integration stage — distinguishing AIPU from generic precision-regulation accounts.
- **Related concepts**: Precision-regulation account, Sustained sensory engagement

## Precision-regulation / aberrant precision account
- **Notation**: —
- **Definition**: Predictive-coding accounts (Lawson et al., 2014; Van de Cruys et al., 2014) attributing autism to atypical estimation/use of precision — overweighting sensory prediction errors, sometimes specifically for surprising events (phasic) or more inflexibly (tonic).
- **Boundary conditions**: Phasic variants predict surprise-graded effects; the present data (interference even under high prevalence) fit these less well than AIPU.
- **Related concepts**: AIPU account, Prediction error

## Sustained sensory engagement
- **Notation**: —
- **Definition**: The proposed mechanism whereby autistic individuals continue accumulating sensory samples after a salient event — manifesting as more fixations and more target refixations — feeding the iterative updating process before the belief stabilises and a response is committed.
- **Boundary conditions**: Inferred interpretive construct linking the AIPU account to the observed eye-movement signatures; not independently measured.
- **Related concepts**: AIPU account, Post-selective processing, Disengagement
