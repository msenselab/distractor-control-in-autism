# Constraints, Assumptions, and Limitations

## Boundary conditions
- Findings apply to adults with confirmed ICD-10 (F84.x) autism without comorbid neurological conditions, vs. screened TD adults; not necessarily to children, or to autism with intellectual disability.
- The paradigm uses a simple, low-clutter additional-singleton display with a single, perfectly valid spatial cue and a binary (low/high) prevalence manipulation.

## Assumptions
- A1: The 100% valid line cue is encoded as a usable spatial prior despite instructions to ignore it (supported indirectly by comparable first-fixation landing).
- A2: 1.5° ROIs validly classify fixations as target/distractor/other.
- A3: Per-participant aggregation + mixed ANOVA adequately models the design; sphericity/normality assumptions of ANOVA hold sufficiently.
- A4: RADS-R screening adequately removes elevated autism-trait TD participants.

## Known limitations (from manuscript Discussion)
- **Moderate sample** (df ≈ 57): adequate for observed effects but possibly underpowered to detect a small Group × Prevalence modulation. The absence of a significant Group × Prevalence interaction is **not** definitive evidence against surprise-grading; ASD descriptive RT interference still varies numerically (Low = 52.9 ms vs High = 72.9 ms).
- **Ecological validity**: a perfectly valid cue and simple display limit generalisation to naturalistic, cluttered scenes with higher target–distractor similarity and ambiguous priors.
- **ADHD comorbidity** not separated; high ASD–ADHD comorbidity means ADHD-related attentional difficulties cannot be ruled out as a contributor.
- **Cross-sectional**: the IU ↔ interference association cannot establish directionality (AIPU → residual uncertainty vs IU → attentional control).
- **No computational model fit**: the AIPU / drift-diffusion interpretation (larger boundary separation, sustained accumulation) is proposed, not modelled here; trial-type-specific drift changes remain an open question.
