# **Appendix** 

**1\. Correlations between distractor interference and symptom severity**    
We examined potential relationships between the extent of distractor interference and participants' trait scores on the Beck's Depression Inventory (BDI), State‑Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI), and Intolerance of Uncertainty (IU), respectively. Specifically, we correlated each questionnaire score with the magnitude of distractor interference (i.e. RT difference between singleton distractor-present and singleton distractor-absent condition) across participants, to see whether any trait measures predict variability in behavioural performance. We additionally considered the contributions of Group and Distractor Prevalence. Several linear models were performed and compared (see **Table A1**), with Model 2 providing the best fit for modeling the relationship between distractor interference and BDI and STAI, respectively. The estimated linear models are:  
	*Interference = 36.184 + 2.927 × BDI − 25.124 × GroupTD − 1.455 × PrevalenceLow* ,		(1)  
	*Interference = 36.121 + 3.851 × STAI-trait − 23.881 × GroupTD − 1.455 × PrevalenceLow* ,	(2)  
where the Group variable was sum contrast coded (TD = −1, ASD = +1), and the Prevalence variable was coded similarly (Low = −1, High = +1). The Trait variable was *z*-scored (M = 0, SD = 1).   

The linear models reveal that Group consistently predicts distractor interference across BDI and STAI analyses (BDI: p = .005; STAI: p = .027). This significant Group effect indicates that the ASD group shows greater distractor interference compared to the TD group, independent of individual differences in depression or trait anxiety scores. The trait predictors themselves (BDI, STAI-Trait, IU) do not show significant relationships with distractor interference after controlling for Group (BDI p = .74, STAI p = .72, IU p = .66), suggesting that within-group variation in these psychological characteristics does not systematically relate to attentional control differences. Similarly, distractor prevalence does not significantly modulate interference effects (all Prevalence p > .84). These findings suggest that group-level differences in attentional control are robust and not explained by comorbid affective symptoms or uncertainty intolerance (**Figure A1**).
