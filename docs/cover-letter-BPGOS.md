## Cover letter draft for BPGOS

Predictive processing models in perception and cognition have traditionally focused on the precision of priors and prediction errors during perceptual inference. In contrast, our fundamental reconceptualization – the Atypical Iterative Prior Updating (AIPU) account - highlights a new perspective to the Bayesian brain framework: the temporal dynamics of belief revision. 

We tested predictions made by previous accounts and the AIPU against each other. In our manuscript, “Persistent Belief Updating Despite Perfect Predictability – the Atypical Iterative Prior Updating Account of Autism,” we disentangle the pre-selective from the post-selective stages of processing. The results converge to prolonged, iterative prior updating that continually reshapes attention and decision making. 

Our findings provide a clear behavioral signature that separates prediction formation from evidence accumulation, challenging existing theories and extending predictive processing models to any domain where perception, attention, and decision making intersect. This reconceptualization builds on a series of our papers, offering a convergent mechanistic account of information processing with broad relevance for understanding Bayesian brain processes beyond clinical populations.

We believe our manuscript will interest the Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging readership because it (i) addresses a fundamental theoretical gap in cognitive neuroscience, (ii) employs a rigorous experimental design, and (iii) advances a general model of human information processing.

Thank you for your consideration.
