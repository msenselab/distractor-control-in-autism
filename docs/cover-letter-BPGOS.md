## Cover letter draft for BPGOS

Predictive processing models in perception and cognition have traditionally focused on the precision of priors and prediction errors during perceptual inference. In contrast, our fundamental reconceptualization – the Atypical Iterative Prior Updating (AIPU) account - highlights a new perspective to the Bayesian brain framework: the temporal dynamics of belief revision. 

We tested predictions made by previous accounts and the AIPU against each other. In our manuscript, “Persistent Belief Updating Despite Perfect Predictability – the Atypical Iterative Prior Updating Account of Autism,” we disentangle the pre-selective from the post-selective stages of processing. The results converge to prolonged, iterative prior updating that continually reshapes attention and decision making. Our findings move the debate from whether autistic individuals can form predictions to how strongly new sensory evidence continues to reshape processing after predictions are already available.

We believe our manuscript fits the scope of Biological Psychiatry: Global Open Science because it extends recent work in the journal on predictive processing in autism, including Shi et al.’s atypical iterative prior updating account and Norrman et al.’s meta-analysis of prediction and prediction error in autism, while connecting this computational framework to attentional control and eye-movement dynamics. Our paper also complements the journal's recent publications on selective attention, sensory processing, and autism-related functional circuitry.

By separating prediction formation from post-selective updating, our findings refine predictive processing accounts of autism and identify a behavioral and oculomotor signature of altered evidence weighting. We think this contribution will interest readers concerned with computational psychiatry, autism, sensory processing, and the mechanisms linking attention to clinical neurodevelopmental phenotypes. 

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
